1 · What changes

  • Today one table holds both companies and people, told apart by a flag.
  • People move to their own table. Companies stay put.
  • Every ID stays the same. Nothing is renumbered, so nothing that points at a person has to be found and rewritten by hand.
  • The table that records which people work with which companies is untouched, which is what keeps one person able to sit at two companies.

2 · The five relations that follow them the real work

Everything else in the database points at a company and does not move. Only these five point at a person.

The tickets one is the problem. 1,794 tickets record who raised them. That link has to be rebuilt, and the tickets table has 39 columns built up over 117 separate changes. Rebuilding it means reproducing all of that exactly. This is the single riskiest step and it is where I will go slowest.

3 · Why this cannot be quiet

The database enforces these links, so they cannot simply be re-pointed. For each of the five, the only way is: build a new table, copy every row across, delete the old one, rename the new one into its place. That is a real operation on live data, done five times. It will be careful and it will be reversible. It will not be invisible, and I would rather tell you that now than discover it halfway through.

5 · The order

  1. The other lane goes first. They are adding your new archived state to accounts. That lands before I touch anything, so a state change and a table split are never in flight together.
  2. The move. New contacts table carrying both states (inactive and archived), 110 people copied across, the five relations rebuilt, then the people removed from the old table.
  3. The code. Around 78 files currently ask "is this row a person or a company?" and each has to ask the right table instead.
  4. The counts. Every badge and number that counts people or companies has to count against the right table. This is the easiest thing to get silently wrong — a wrong count looks perfectly normal.
  5. Proof. Every relation read back and compared against what it said before the move, then deployed to staging.

6 · How it is undone

A full snapshot of every affected table is written before the first change, exactly as the accounts import did, with a one-command restore. At every step the old data still exists until the new data has been read back and proved correct.
